1. What “public domain” means here

The project’s character references concern works whose U.S. copyrights have expired or otherwise ended, including early published appearances. That does not put every depiction of a character into the public domain. Later drawings, traits, stories, recordings, restorations with new creative material, and branding can have separate protection. A 1930s/1940s visual style does not establish permission to copy everything from that era.

As of 2026, the U.S. Copyright Office explains that works published in the United States before January 1, 1931 are in the U.S. public domain. Copyright terms and other legal protections differ internationally. The website’s availability in another country is not a representation that the same rights analysis applies there.

This page describes the project’s intended reference basis. It is not an independent clearance certificate, legal opinion, or promise that every finished design or use has been legally reviewed. A disclaimer does not cure copying of protected later material or misleading branding.

3. Popeye’s spinach feature

Popeye’s 1929 debut and his later spinach-associated strength are different source questions. Enlarged forearms and an anchor tattoo already appear in the original 1929 depiction. The current game includes a spinach power-up with an independently drawn bunch of leaves, rather than a canned-spinach film prop. Duke’s researchers report that the relevant 1931 comic strip’s copyright appears not to have been renewed; they qualify that finding rather than presenting it as certain. Ordinary term expiration for those 1931 materials occurs on January 1, 2027 in the U.S.

The project has not performed an independent renewal search. Its spinach mechanic relies on Duke’s published research, not an assertion that every Popeye feature appeared in 1929 or that the later animated cartoons are public domain. Changing the prop does not resolve that separate source question. This limitation remains relevant to distribution and reuse.

5. Game assets, authorship & reuse

The character animation is script-authored digital vector cel-style artwork made for the game, not extracted historic film frames or hand-painted traditional cels. Stage paintings were generated for the project, with separate Canvas animation. Music and sound effects are original software synthesis, not historic recordings, sampled cartoon voices, or recordings of live musicians. Ending stories were written for this game.

Sources and creation methods are documented in the repository’s asset provenance record. Creating a new drawing does not by itself establish clearance of an underlying character. Generated imagery is not represented here as automatically copyrightable or free of all possible rights claims.

No exclusive rights are claimed in underlying public-domain material. Newly contributed material and third-party components may have separate rights or license terms. A publicly accessible GitHub repository and the words “Public Domain” in the game’s title do not, by themselves, place all source code, art, or music in the public domain. Follow any applicable license for the particular material; this page does not grant additional licenses or warrant rights for redistribution or commercial use.

6. Playing the game

This is fictional cartoon combat for entertainment. It includes stylized fighting, flashing impact effects, moving backgrounds, and sound. Adjust sound and use your device’s reduced-motion preference as appropriate. Reduced motion limits ambient animation and camera shake; combat still has moving and changing images. Stop playing if the effects cause discomfort.

The game has no official age-rating certification stated here. Parents and guardians should consider its content and supervise links to external services. Gameplay is not instruction or encouragement to imitate violence in real life. The privacy policy explains local saves, network providers, and privacy choices.
